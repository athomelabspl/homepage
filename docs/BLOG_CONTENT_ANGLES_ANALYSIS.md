## Part A: Situational angles

These are breakup scenarios where the user's *context* shapes what they need, not just their emotional state. Each creates a distinct reader moment that maps naturally to app features.

---

### A1. The long-term relationship breakup

**Why it's different:** Years of intertwined routines, shared identity, maybe shared housing or finances. The grief is partly practical, not just emotional. People expect to feel worse for longer — and usually do.

**App connection:** Daily structure replaces the routine that disappeared. The journal's device-only privacy matters when someone is in a shared space mid-split. Bamboo growth milestones acknowledge time as meaningful without promising a calendar.

**Key content:** Self-concept clarity research (Slotter et al., 2010); why recovery takes longer when lives are intertwined; small structure during chaos; what "getting back to yourself" actually means when you shared who you were.

**CTA angle:** "A short daily routine that doesn't require a plan" — speaks directly to the chaos of a long-term split.

**SEO angle:** "getting over a long-term relationship," "how to heal after years together"

---

### A2. Breakup when you still love them (not mutual)

**Why it's different:** The person who was left — no narrative of the other person being "wrong," love intact, no anger to fuel distance. One of the most painful and misrepresented scenarios online.

**App connection:** Reconnector and Storm Rider types map closely to this. Affirmations and self-validation challenges (from Reddit doc) are crucial here, not no-contact "rules." Today's Pulse validation lines ("You are worthy of love and healing").

**Key content:** Difference between grief and heartbreak; why closure you don't get still has to be built yourself; self-closure challenges from the app; self-compassion (day challenge: write what you'd say to a friend).

**CTA angle:** "Support that doesn't ask you to stop loving them" — validates rather than corrects.

**SEO angle:** "how to get over someone you still love," "breakup when you're still in love"

---

### A3. Breakup after infidelity

**Why it's different:** Two layers of grief: the relationship and the betrayal. Anger that can flip to self-blame. Trust in yourself damaged alongside trust in them. Shame and confusion about why you still miss them.

**App connection:** Storm Rider challenges (unsent letter, body scan, action over analysis). No-contact framing as "protecting your peace" lands differently here — framed as boundary not punishment. Breathing / support hub for the spikes.

**Key content:** Why betrayal grief is specifically complicated; the self-blame loop and what feeds it; anger as information; why you can grieve someone who hurt you.

**CTA angle:** "Support that doesn't judge the mess" — validates that grief after betrayal isn't straightforward.

**SEO angle:** "how to get over a breakup after cheating," "breakup after infidelity"

---

### A4. Breakup when you were the one who ended it

**Why it's different:** No cultural permission to be sad. People expect relief; they often get grief, guilt, and doubt instead. "You chose this" makes the pain feel illegitimate.

**App connection:** Reset Builder type maps closely — numb, doubting, low motivation despite having made the decision. Reframe challenges ("This relationship taught me…"). No-contact is still relevant but for a different reason.

**Key content:** Why the person who ends it also grieves; the "did I make a mistake" loop and what it usually is; guilt vs grief; self-trust after a hard decision.

**CTA angle:** "For when you're the one who said it's over — and it still hurts."

**SEO angle:** "grieving a breakup you chose," "feeling sad after a breakup you initiated," "why do I miss them if I broke up with them"

---

### A5. Breakup when you live together / shared a home

**Why it's different:** Logistics make emotional distance almost impossible in the immediate term. Every object is a trigger. Someone may have to move.

**App connection:** Environment reset challenges (one object, new route, different bedside setup). The daily routine the app provides replaces the domestic one. Journal as private space when you share a home.

**Key content:** How to create emotional distance inside a shared space; the object-memory link; when to do the physical cleanup; protecting routines before the physical separation happens.

**CTA angle:** "A private space that isn't the flat you're trying to leave."

**SEO angle:** "breaking up when living together," "how to cope with a breakup when you still live together"

---

### A6. Breakup and anxiety spirals (3am thoughts)

**Why it's different:** People often search at night or during a panic wave, not during calm research sessions. This article meets them exactly where they are.

**App connection:** Support hub — "Help me right now" — is the core feature this article explains. Breathing (4-1-5), quick journal, distraction menu. Today's Pulse as a morning anchor. Connects directly to the anxious / Storm Rider experience.

**Key content:** What anxiety after a breakup actually is (nervous system, not weakness); why nights are harder; the 4-1-5 breath and why it works; one-action interrupts for spirals.

**CTA angle:** "Something steady when a wave hits at 3am." This can directly echo the app's "Calm down first" welcome button.

**SEO angle:** "breakup anxiety at night," "3am thoughts after breakup," "breakup panic attack"

---

### A7. When your ex has moved on (and you haven't)

**Why it's different:** Triggers a specific second grief — not just the loss, but the social comparison. Social media makes this unavoidable for most people.

**App connection:** Digital boundary challenges (mute/unfollow ex's network, scroll limit). No-contact redefined: your peace doesn't depend on their timeline. Reframe challenge: "This relationship taught me…"

**Key content:** Why seeing your ex move on triggers a grief spike even months in; the social comparison loop; digital boundaries as self-protection not blocking-in-anger; your recovery timeline vs theirs.

**CTA angle:** "Your timeline doesn't depend on theirs."

**SEO angle:** "my ex has already moved on," "how to cope when your ex is dating someone new"

---

### A8. Breakup and loss of identity / friend group

**Why it's different:** Beyond the relationship, you lose the social ecosystem built around it — mutual friends, couple routines, in-laws you liked. Identity is fractured on multiple levels.

**App connection:** Self-concept clarity research (Slotter et al., 2010) is foundational here. Reconnector challenges (reach out, schedule connection) and self-closure journal prompts ("What did this teach me about my needs?"). Bamboo milestone: rebuilding who you are day by day.

**Key content:** Why breakups shrink your sense of self not just your social life; the friend-group split; rebuilding an identity that isn't defined by the relationship; "future self" writing exercise.

**CTA angle:** "For when you lost more than the relationship."

**SEO angle:** "losing your friend group after a breakup," "breakup identity loss," "who am I after a breakup"

---

### A9. Breakup and low motivation / can't function

**Why it's different:** Behavioral shutdown — not just sadness but inability to eat, work, exercise, reply to messages. People Googling this are hitting a floor.

**App connection:** Reset Builder is the exact type for this. The app's whole design — 3 small challenges, no judgment for not completing all, "you showing up today counts" — is built for low-activation states. Gentle step acknowledgements. "Fresh start. Today counts."

**Key content:** What low-motivation after a breakup is (not laziness, not depression necessarily); how grief can show up as flatness; the behavioral activation principle (small action → slightly better state); why completion doesn't have to mean full compliance.

**CTA angle:** "Built for days when you can't do much. Three small things. No pressure."

**SEO angle:** "can't function after a breakup," "breakup depression motivation," "how to get out of bed after a breakup"

---

### A10. No contact when you share kids or work together

**Why it's different:** Full no contact is impossible. People who search this have already been told "just block them" and know it doesn't apply to them. They feel excluded from the standard advice.

**App connection:** `should-you-do-no-contact.mdx` covers this briefly. There's room for a standalone piece. App: functional/boundaried communication; journal as private processing space separate from co-parenting logistics; support hub for when a work encounter or handover leaves them activated.

**Key content:** Functional contact vs emotional contact; the script for co-parenting exchanges; how to protect emotional distance inside necessary contact; what no contact means when "no contact" is impossible.

**CTA angle:** "For when no contact isn't an option — but you still need your own space."

**SEO angle:** "no contact when you have kids," "no contact when you work together," "low contact after breakup"

## Part B: Personality-type (attachment-style) articles

These directly explain what makes each recovery type different — educational, builds trust, drives quiz completion.

**Rules for this cluster:**
- Never diagnose. "Attachment-inspired coping style," not "you have anxious attachment disorder."
- Each article educates the reader about their own pattern, then shows how the app was built around it.
- Frame as self-knowledge, not a label. "This might be you" not "this is what you are."

---

### B1. Anxious attachment and breakups — the Reconnector

**Core reader experience:** Can't stop checking their profile. Keeps replaying the last conversation. Needs closure, one more talk, proof it was real. Feels pathetic for caring this much.

**What the article covers:**
- What anxious attachment looks like in a breakup (without pathologizing)
- Why the pull to reach out is so physical, not just emotional (nervous system regulation; Sbarra & Hazan, 2008)
- The reassurance-seeking loop and why it doesn't close
- Self-closure: building your own version without their words
- Self-validation: what you gave; that it mattered without their acknowledgment

**App connection:** Reconnector quiz result; challenges built for redirecting connection inward (reach out to a friend, self-compassion writing); affirmations for worth-without-approval; no-contact tracker as tool for the "reaching out" urge.

**Key copy:** "You keep reaching for them because that was how your nervous system regulated. That's not weakness. It's a pattern that can shift."

**SEO angle:** "anxious attachment breakup," "why I can't stop thinking about my ex," "need closure after breakup"

---

### B2. Avoidant attachment and breakups — the Guarded

**Core reader experience:** Looks fine from the outside. Tells people they're okay. Then six months later crashes for no obvious reason. Struggles to ask for help or understand why they feel worse later.

**What the article covers:**
- What avoidant coping looks like after a breakup (distance, busyness, delayed grief)
- Why it often feels like you're over it before you are
- The emotional rebound risk — grief parked vs grief done
- Why "I'm fine" can become its own trap
- Gradual emotional awareness: reflection at your own pace, not on-demand vulnerability

**App connection:** Guarded quiz result; safe reflection prompts at their own pace; challenges that don't push vulnerability (digital boundary, "no rush" day, write why you're waiting); app as low-pressure daily touchpoint.

**Key copy:** "Pulling away is one way of protecting yourself. It works until it doesn't. The grief usually catches up."

**SEO angle:** "avoidant attachment breakup," "why I'm not sad after breakup," "feeling nothing after a breakup"

---

### B3. Emotional overwhelm and breakups — the Storm Rider

**Core reader experience:** Mood swings between okay and devastated. Hard evenings. Feels crazy for how intense it is. Maybe made some impulsive decisions (texted something they regret, blocked and unblocked).

**What the article covers:**
- What emotional dysregulation in breakup grief looks like
- Why intensity is common and doesn't mean something is wrong
- Nights being harder than days (stimulation drop)
- Grounding vs suppressing: feeling the wave without being swallowed
- Physical tools: breathing, movement, body scan
- Structure as nervous system support, not control

**App connection:** Storm Rider quiz result; breathing (4-1-5) in the support hub; movement challenges; unsent letter; body scan; 3-challenge daily structure as an anchor for high-reactivity days.

**Key copy:** "The swings make sense. When your nervous system is activated, the swings are larger. The work is finding anchors, not a flat line."

**SEO angle:** "emotional breakdown after breakup," "mood swings after breakup," "intense grief after breakup," "can't control emotions after breakup"

---

### B4. Emotional numbness and shutdown — the Reset Builder

**Core reader experience:** Everyone says this should be devastating. They agree it mattered. And yet: nothing. Or very little. Flat. Wondering if they're a sociopath or just broken.

**What the article covers:**
- Numbness as a grief response, not a character flaw
- How the nervous system shuts down when overloaded (polyvagal framing, hedged)
- Why feelings often arrive later as the numbness fades
- The risk of mistaking shutdown for recovery
- Gentle activation: tiny tasks, sensory engagement, low-stakes structure

**App connection:** Reset Builder quiz result; small daily challenges designed specifically for flat states ("gentle activation"); "Fresh start. Today counts." copy; bamboo growth as evidence of showing up without requiring feeling-based proof.

**Note:** There is already a published article on feeling numb. This article goes further — connecting the numbness to a broader shutdown/avoidance coping pattern across the recovery timeline, not just the first weeks.

**Key copy:** "Flat isn't the same as fine. It also isn't broken. It's one way a mind absorbs more than it can feel all at once."

**SEO angle:** "why am I numb after breakup," "can't feel anything after breakup," "shutdown after breakup"

---

### B5. Mixed types: when you're both anxious and overwhelmed

**Core reader experience:** Seeks reassurance and also has emotional swings. Reaches out impulsively then regrets it. Feels both the pull to reconnect and the chaos of being unable to regulate.

**What the article covers:**
- How secondary types work (primary + secondary quiz outcome)
- What it looks like to have both Reconnector and Storm Rider tendencies
- Why one pattern shows up early (acute phase) and the other later
- Tools from both: grounding for the waves, self-closure for the seeking

**App connection:** Secondary type system in quiz results; personalized challenges that blend personality tags; today's pulse as daily calibration.

**Key copy:** "Some days the urge to reach out wins. Some days it's the wave. Often it's both. Neither one has to mean anything about who you are."

**SEO angle:** "anxious attachment mood swings," "overwhelmed and needy after breakup," "why do I keep texting then regretting it"

## Rules for all new articles

These apply in addition to the global standards in `BREAKUP_RESET_CONTENT_BRIEFS.md`:

1. **Lead with the reader's moment**, not the feature list. Person first.
2. **One soft app mention per article max** — never more than one sentence. The `<ArticlePromo />` handles conversion.
3. **No quiz outcomes as diagnoses.** "If you recognize yourself in this" not "if you are a Reconnector."
4. **Personality articles should be useful without the app** — they stand alone as breakup education.
5. **Situational articles should acknowledge variance** — not everyone leaving a long-term relationship is a certain type; not everyone post-infidelity is angry.
6. **No overlap with published articles without clear differentiation** — check against the 14 existing articles before writing.
7. **Science hedging always** — no "studies prove," always "research suggests" or "many people."
8. **Avoid in today's/in recent years openers** — already on the AI phrase list.
