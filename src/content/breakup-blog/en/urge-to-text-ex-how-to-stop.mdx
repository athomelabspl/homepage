You have a message typed out. Your heart is racing. You are staring at the screen and looking for an excuse to hit send.

Stop. Do not delete the text yet, but do not send it. Just put the phone down. Give yourself exactly ten minutes before you make a decision.

You are in a state of acute emotional urgency. Your brain is tricking you into thinking this text is a matter of survival. Here is how to navigate the next ten minutes without making yourself feel worse.

## The Science of the Urge

You might feel weak or pathetic for wanting to reach out. You are neither of those things.

The craving to text your ex is a physical reality. When you were together, your partner helped regulate your nervous system. A text from them provided a hit of dopamine and lowered your stress hormones.

Now that they are gone, your brain still craves that regulation. When you feel lonely, panicked, or sad, your body remembers what used to bring relief. Your brain is treating a text message to your ex like a survival mechanism. Recognizing this as a chemical craving takes the shame out of the moment.

For more on riding out those waves over days and weeks, [keeping no contact when it feels impossible](/breakup-reset/blog/en/keeping-no-contact-after-breakup/) covers what the urge is doing and why it usually peaks, then fades.

## The Closure Trap

Your mind is a brilliant negotiator. It will try to convince you that this text is completely logical.

You might tell yourself that you just need to clarify one last thing. You might think an apology will fix the heavy feeling in your chest. You might believe this one text will finally give you closure.

This is the closure trap. Sending the text will not bring relief.

The moment you hit send, your anxiety will not disappear. It will simply change shape. You will enter a new cycle of waiting. You will stare at read receipts. You will analyze their punctuation. If they do not reply, you will feel crushed. The text is not a solution. It is just a different kind of pain.

## The 10-Minute Protocol

You cannot think your way out of a panic spike. You have to use physical actions to break the thought loop. Follow these three steps right now.

### 1. Change Your Physical Environment

Take your phone and place it face down in another room. Walk away from it. If you are sitting on the couch, move to the kitchen. If you are in bed, stand up and walk to the window. Changing your physical location forces your brain to process new visual information.

### 2. Change Your Body Temperature

Extreme temperature shifts can help reset your nervous system. Go to the bathroom and splash cold water on your face. Hold an ice cube in your hand until it melts. This physical sensation distracts your brain from the emotional panic.

### 3. Write It Somewhere Else

You still have words you desperately need to say. You just cannot send them to your ex. Grab a piece of paper. Write down everything you wanted to text them. Write down how angry you are. Write down how much you miss them. Get the words out of your body and onto a safe, private page.
