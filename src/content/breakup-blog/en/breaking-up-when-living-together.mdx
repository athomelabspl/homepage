<AISummary>
- Shift your dynamic from romantic partners to business roommates as soon as you can.
- Separate sleeping arrangements to avoid painful emotional confusion.
- Coordinate schedules so you can have time alone in the shared space.
- Find a strictly private place to process your feelings where they cannot see it.
</AISummary>

The relationship is over. But you still have to figure out who is buying paper towels.

Standard breakup advice tells you to create distance and cut contact. That is frustrating to read when you share a kitchen and a lease. Living with an ex is a specific kind of exhaustion. You feel like a stranger in your own home. Your system stays on edge.

Here is how to survive this gray area until one of you can move out.

## The Roommate Shift

You need to change the dynamic. You are no longer romantic partners. You are business roommates.

That means separating your lives as much as the physical space allows. Start with sleeping arrangements tonight. Even if it means taking the couch, do it. Physical proximity breeds emotional confusion. Sleeping in the same bed makes it harder for your brain to understand the breakup is real.

Next, divide household responsibilities. Stop doing their laundry. Stop cooking dinner for two. You are only responsible for yourself now.

If full silence is impossible because you share a home, [how to think about no contact when you are not sure](/breakup-reset/blog/en/should-you-do-no-contact/) can help you sort low contact from wishful closeness.

## Creating Distance in a Shared Home

You cannot heal if your environment constantly reminds you of what you lost. You need to reset your space.

### Remove the Visual Triggers

Box up the shared photos. Hide the sentimental items. You do not have to throw them away, but you should not have to look at them every day. Small changes help. Move furniture around. Change the setup of your bedside table. Break the visual routines you built as a couple.

### The Schedule Hack

Coordinate your routines to minimize overlap. If they are watching television in the living room, go to your room or take a walk. Treat shared areas like a shift schedule. You both deserve moments where you feel completely alone in the house to decompress.

## Protecting Your Privacy

The hardest part of living together is the lack of privacy. You cannot cry freely in the living room. You might feel like you are being watched or judged.

You need a safe place to process anger and grief. When you share a home, a physical journal can feel risky. Leaving a notebook on the kitchen counter is a vulnerability you may not want right now.

If you need a private outlet, Breakup Reset includes a daily journal stored on your device, not in the cloud. It gives you a private place to vent and sort your next steps without worrying that someone else will find it. Small environment resets help too: one bedside corner that is yours, one routine that does not involve them.
